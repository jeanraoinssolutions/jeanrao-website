ACA Income & Subsidies

HealthCare.gov financial assistance—including Premium Tax Credits (APTC) and Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR)—is based on your estimated household Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) for the coverage year.

How ACA Calculates Your Household Income

For Marketplace purposes, MAGI starts with your Federal Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) from your tax return, then adds back three specific non-taxable items if applicable:

  • Nontaxable Social Security benefits
  • Tax-exempt interest income
  • Untaxed foreign income

Household Income Calculation Breakdown

ACA Marketplace financial assistance is based on your household's estimated income and tax situation. The examples below are general educational examples, not a tax determination of whether a particular payment must be included.

Generally Counts as Household Income Examples That May Not Count the Same Way
Wages, salaries, tips, and taxable commissions Qualified Roth IRA distributions
Net self-employment income (business profit) Return of your own investment basis
Taxable interest and tax-exempt interest Certain loan proceeds (e.g., student loans, personal loans)
Ordinary dividends and taxable capital gains Gifts and inheritances
Traditional IRA distributions & 401(k)/retirement payouts Certain qualified Health Savings Account (HSA) distributions
Pensions and taxable retirement benefits Child support payments received
Social Security benefits (taxable and nontaxable portions) Supplemental Security Income (SSI)
Net rental income, royalties, and farming income Certain government assistance programs (e.g., SNAP/Food Stamps)
Unemployment compensation Reimbursements for qualified expenses

Two Types of Financial Assistance

  • Advance Premium Tax Credit (APTC): Lowers your monthly health insurance premium payments throughout the year.
  • Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR): Available on Silver plans for lower-income tiers, reducing your out-of-pocket costs like copays, deductibles, and coinsurance.
