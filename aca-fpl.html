Federal Poverty Level (FPL) Guidelines for Texas

HealthCare.gov uses annual Federal Poverty Level (FPL) income thresholds to measure your eligibility for Marketplace premium subsidies (APTC) and Cost-Sharing Reductions (CSR).

Important: These are approximate reference figures for the federal poverty guidelines for the 48 contiguous states and D.C. (which includes Texas). Actual Marketplace financial assistance depends on the applicable coverage year, household size, estimated household income, tax household, state, and other eligibility requirements. Alaska and Hawaii have different FPL amounts. This table is for general educational purposes and is not an eligibility determination.

Important: FPL is NOT the Only Subsidy Requirement

Falling within an income range relative to the FPL does not automatically guarantee a subsidy. FPL is only one factor in the overall Premium Tax Credit (APTC) calculation. To qualify for Marketplace financial help, you must also meet several other federal criteria:

  • Marketplace Enrollment: You must enroll through HealthCare.gov in a qualified health plan.
  • No Affordable Employer Coverage: You (and your dependents) must not have access to an employer-sponsored health plan that meets minimum value standards and is considered affordable under IRS guidelines.
  • No Other Qualifying Government Coverage: You must not be eligible for Medicare, Medicaid, CHIP, or TRICARE.
  • Tax Filing Status: If married, you must generally file a joint tax return to claim premium tax credits. You must also agree to reconcile your advance subsidies on your federal income tax return.

Texas Specific Rules & Timing Details

Texas Medicaid Gap (Under 100% FPL)

Because Texas has not expanded Medicaid, non-disabled adults earning under 100% FPL generally do not qualify for Medicaid or Marketplace subsidies. Marketplace subsidy eligibility in Texas begins at 100% FPL ($15,960 for an individual).

How FPL Timing Works for Plan Years

For Premium Tax Credit purposes, the applicable FPL guideline is based on the most recently published FPL figures available on the first day of the annual Open Enrollment Period. For example, Open Enrollment for the coverage year relies on the federally published HHS poverty guidelines at that time. When preparing for upcoming plan years, we ensure calculations use the exact federally mandated guidelines for that specific enrollment cycle.
